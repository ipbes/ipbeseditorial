# IPBES editorial house style: full rules

These rules are for English IPBES documents: Plenary reports, pre-session documents, decisions, information documents and related texts.

**Principle.** The UN Editorial Manual (DGACM) is the baseline. IPBES differs from it only where the IPBES Plenary reports show a **deliberate and consistent** practice. The evidence comes from the 12 Plenary reports IPBES/1 to IPBES/12, 2013–2026; see `evidence.md`. Each deviation below is marked **IPBES practice**. Where the reports are inconsistent, the UN rule applies.

Precedence when sources disagree:

1. IPBES practice listed here.
2. UN Editorial Manual: https://www.un.org/dgacm/en/content/editorial-manual
3. UN spelling list (`un-spelling-list.md`), then the online Oxford Dictionary (British English).
4. UNTERM (https://unterm.un.org) for names and terms not covered here.

**Every document is open for editing.** The secretariat edits a document and then sends it to UNDOCS for final editing and publishing. The goal is to send clean documents. Nothing is edited after UNDOCS has processed it, so treat every document as a draft that has not yet been submitted. That includes Plenary reports, decisions and annexes. Apply the rules for style, spelling, punctuation, terms and format to the whole document. Do not hold back because a text has been agreed or adopted. What does not change is the substance: what was said, decided, requested or approved, and every figure, name, date and symbol.

---

## 1. Voice

IPBES is **policy-relevant but not policy-prescriptive**. This is the rule that matters most.

- **Present evidence, options and findings. Do not tell Governments or other actors what they must do.** Prefer "options include", "could", "may", "the assessment found that". Avoid "must", "should", "need to" and "it is essential that" in IPBES-authored narrative.
- **Keep prescriptive wording where it belongs:**
  - Quote or report it when it is a speaker's view: "the representative said that Governments should…".
  - Keep it in the operative text of decisions, where it is the substance: "*Requests* the Executive Secretary…".
- **Be neutral, factual and measured.** No advocacy, no slogans, no superlatives such as "groundbreaking" or "unprecedented", unless the speaker used them.
- **Represent views in a balanced way.**
  - Attribute a view to the speaker or group that expressed it: "one representative", "several representatives", "the representative of X, speaking on behalf of the Group of African States".
  - Never present one member's view as the Plenary's view.
- **Do not change meaning.** Never invent or alter figures, names, dates, quotes, symbols or decisions. If something is missing or doubtful, flag it.
- **Use plain, precise language** within the conventions of an intergovernmental report. Most of the formulaic wording is deliberate; see `report-structure.md`.

## 2. Countries and regions

- **Use the English short names from the IPBES geography list** (`countries.csv`, from https://ibok.ipbes.net/rest/v1/geo/data?format=application/rdf%2Bxml). They follow UNTERM.
  - Drop the trailing "(the)" marker in lists. Add "the" in running text where English grammar needs it: "the Russian Federation", "the representative of the Republic of Korea".
  - Keep inverted qualifiers in lists: "Netherlands (Kingdom of the)", "Bolivia (Plurinational State of)". The reports write "the Kingdom of the Netherlands" only when reporting what a representative said.
- **Names to check.** Correct forms used in IPBES reports: Türkiye (not Turkey); Czechia; Eswatini; North Macedonia; Cabo Verde; Côte d’Ivoire (curly apostrophe); Viet Nam; Lao People’s Democratic Republic; Republic of Korea; Democratic People’s Republic of Korea; Russian Federation; Syrian Arab Republic; United Republic of Tanzania; Republic of Moldova; Bolivia (Plurinational State of); Iran (Islamic Republic of); Venezuela (Bolivarian Republic of); Netherlands (Kingdom of the).
- **United Kingdom and United States.** Give the full name, "United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland" or "United States of America", at first mention. Use "United Kingdom" or "United States" after that (UN short title rule). Both forms are used consistently in the reports.
- **Order.** List countries in English alphabetical order, as in the reports' lists of members and of credentials.
- **Members and regions.**
  - IPBES has "members" (States members of IPBES): "members of the Platform", "IPBES members". Do not write "Member States" for IPBES members. "Member States" means States Members of the United Nations.
  - Regional groupings follow the UN regional groups: African States; Asia-Pacific States; Eastern European States; Latin American and Caribbean States; Western European and other States.
  - IPBES subregions are listed in `countries.csv`.
- **Status checks.** Use `countries.csv` to check whether a country is an IPBES member (`ipbes_member`, `member_since`). Do not change membership statements in a report. Flag any inconsistency.
- **Maps.** Boundaries, names and designations need the standard UN disclaimer and clearance (see section 5.2).

## 3. Inclusive language

See `inclusive-language.md`. In short:

- **Gender.**
  - Use "Chair", "Co-Chair" and "Vice-Chair", never "Chairman".
  - Use "spokesperson" and "humankind".
  - Use "Ms." unless another title is known. Never use "Mrs.", "Dr." or "H.E.".
  - **Names (current IPBES practice since IPBES-10).** At first mention, give the full name without a courtesy title, followed by the post: "Anne Larigauderie, IPBES Executive Secretary"; "the Chair of IPBES, David Obura". After that, use "Ms." or "Mr." and the surname: "Ms. Larigauderie". Reports up to IPBES-9 used "Ms. Anne Larigauderie"; do not change older documents.
  - Prefer neutral constructions: plurals, "who", omitting the possessive.
  - Avoid "he or she" unless it is needed.
  - Treat women and men in parallel, for example the same form of name and title.
- **Disability.** Use people-first terms, "persons with disabilities". Avoid "the disabled", "suffers from" and "wheelchair-bound".
- **Indigenous Peoples.**
  - Capitalize "Indigenous" for peoples, cultures, knowledge and communities: "Indigenous Peoples and local communities", "Indigenous and local knowledge". This is the UN rule and IPBES practice since IPBES-10.
  - Lower case "indigenous" for species ("indigenous species").

## 4. Style

### 4.1 Abbreviations and acronyms

UN rules:
- **First mention.** Spell out in full at first mention in the main text, with the abbreviation in parentheses. Use the abbreviation only if the term recurs.
- **Where abbreviations go.**
  - Not in titles or headings.
  - Spell an abbreviation out again in the main text if it first appeared only in a footnote, table or figure.
- **Form.**
  - No full stops: "UNEP", not "U.N.E.P.".
  - No "the" before an acronym used as a noun ("members of UNESCO"), but "the" is fine when it is adjectival ("the IPBES secretariat").
  - No possessive "’s" on an acronym or a body's name: "the UNEP Executive Director", not "UNEP's".
  - Do not abbreviate "United Nations" in running text.
- **"e.g." and "i.e."**
  - Use them only inside parentheses. In running text write "for example", "that is" or "namely".
  - **IPBES practice:** add a comma after them: "(e.g., workshops, webinars)", "(i.e., the summary for policymakers)". The reports use "e.g.," 158 times against "e.g." 3 times, and "i.e.," 27 times against 0 (see `evidence.md`). The UN Manual has no comma.
- **No courtesy honorifics.** Never "H.E.", "His Excellency" or "Dr." (unless the person is acting in a medical capacity).
- **References to parts of documents.**
  - Abbreviate only in footnotes and references: chap., para., paras., p., pp., sect., vol., No.
  - Never abbreviate annex, figure, part, document, resolution or decision.

IPBES practice:
- **IPBES itself.** Write "Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services (IPBES)" at first mention in the body. **Never shorten the full name** to "Intergovernmental Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services", or drop or alter "Science-Policy" in any way ("Science Policy", "science-policy"). Correct this wherever it appears, except in direct quotations of other bodies' titles, which are flagged instead. After that, use "IPBES" or "the Platform"; both are used. "The IPBES secretariat", "IPBES members", "IPBES assessments", "IPBES Executive Secretary" and "Chair of IPBES" are all used. "IPBES-12" is used to refer to a session; "the twelfth session of the Plenary" is used in narrative.
- **Assessment names.**
  - Give the full title at first mention, followed by the short name in parentheses, in lower case: "…methodological assessment of the impact and dependence of business on biodiversity and nature’s contributions to people (business and biodiversity assessment)".
  - Use the short name after that.
  - The three-letter codes (BBA, TCA, NXS…) are **internal only**. They never appear in Plenary reports. Do not use them in documents. See `ipbes-terms.md` for all full titles and short names.
  - The short name of the spatial planning assessment is "spatial planning and connectivity assessment" (confirmed by the secretariat). Correct "spatial planning assessment" wherever it appears.
- **United Nations entities.** Spell out at first mention: Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO); United Nations Development Programme (UNDP); United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP); United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO).
- **Other bodies.** Convention on Biological Diversity, Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) and Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework are written in full. Abbreviate them only if they recur and you have defined them.

### 4.2 Capitalization

UN rules:
- **Use capitals sparingly.** Capitals are not a mark of respect.
- **Headings and titles.**
  - Headings, and titles of documents, tables and figures: capitalize the first word and proper nouns only.
  - Titles of books and publications: capitalize the main words.
- **Governments.** "Government" is capitalized when it means a national Government, singular or plural: "Governments", "the Government of Japan". Lower case for local government and for "government department".
- **Short titles** of specific bodies are capitalized: "the Assembly", "the Committee", "the Convention".
- **Lower case:**
  - "annex", "chapter", "section", "paragraph", "article", "appendix", "decision", "resolution", "agenda item 7", "report of the Executive Secretary", "the secretariat" (only the UN Secretariat is capitalized).
- **Officials and posts.**
  - Specific officials are capitalized: "the Executive Secretary", "the Chair", "the Co-Chairs", "Vice-Chair", "Rapporteur".
  - Generic references are lower case: "a chair".
  - "Co-Chair(s)" is capitalized for specific people: "the Co-Chairs of the assessment", "the Co-Chairs of working group II". In generic lists of roles it is lower case: "the co-chairs, coordinating lead authors and lead authors". The verb is "co-chaired". This has been current practice since IPBES-11; older reports used lower case throughout.
- **Geography.** Compass points are lower case unless part of a recognized region: "West Africa", "the Southern Ocean", "northern hemisphere".
- **Other words.**
  - "Earth" is capitalized for the planet.
  - "Internet" is capitalized; "web" and "website" are lower case.
  - "Indigenous" is capitalized for peoples, cultures and knowledge (see section 3).

IPBES practice (consistent in all 12 reports):
- **Bodies.** "the Plenary" (capitalized; UN lists "plenary" in lower case, but at IPBES "Plenary" is the name of the governing body), "the Platform", "the Bureau", "the Multidisciplinary Expert Panel", "the Panel".
- **Lower case:**
  - "the secretariat"
  - "task force(s)"; "the task force on…"
  - "technical support unit"
  - "trust fund"
  - "work programme", "rolling work programme of the Platform up to 2030"
  - "summary for policymakers", "summaries for policymakers"
  - "nature’s contributions to people"
  - "ecosystem services"
  - "members of the Platform", "observers"
  - "the contact group on budget"
- **Assessment names** are lower case in running text, after the first word: "the transformative change assessment", "the nexus assessment", "the second global assessment".

### 4.3 Indirect or reported speech

Plenary reports are in reported speech, in the past tense. Do not use quotation marks for reported statements.

- **Tense shifts:**
  - present → past
  - present perfect → past perfect
  - past → past perfect
  - will → would
  - shall → should
  - will have → would have
  - Leave unchanged: past perfect, "would", "must", "should" and "ought to".
- **Pronoun and place/time shifts:**
  - I/we → he/she/they
  - my/our → his/her/their
  - this → that
  - these → those
  - here → there
  - now → then (keep "now" when it marks a change of circumstances)
  - today → that day
  - tomorrow → the following day
  - yesterday → the previous day
  - ago → earlier
  - next week → the following week
- **Introduce once.** Use one reporting verb at the start, such as "said", "noted", "emphasized", "expressed appreciation", "highlighted", "recalled", "underscored" or "welcomed". Do not repeat "he said that" in every sentence; the tense carries the reporting.
- **Questions** become statements: "asked whether…", ending with a full stop.
- **Universal truths.** These may keep the present tense in reports. Summary records always shift.
- **Proceedings and outcomes.** Reported proceedings are in the past tense. Agreed outcomes are set off under a heading, or as "The Plenary adopted decision IPBES-12/1…".
- **Short passages from earlier decisions or documents** are paraphrased indirectly, without quotation marks.

IPBES practice (standard formulas, all reports): see `report-structure.md` section 4. Examples: "Statements were delivered by…", "The Plenary took note of…", "The Plenary agreed to establish a contact group…", "The Plenary adopted decision IPBES-12/1…", "Representatives expressed appreciation…".

### 4.4 Italics and bold

UN rules:
- **Italicize:**
  - titles of publications, books, periodicals, films and reports when they are cited as publications
  - scientific names at genus and species level (*Apis mellifera*); not at family level and above
  - foreign words that are not in the Oxford Dictionary or the UN spelling list (*inter alia* is in the dictionary, so leave it in roman type, and use it sparingly)
  - variables
- **Do not italicize:**
  - for emphasis
  - non-English names of organizations
  - quotations
- **Bold** is for headings only. In running text, bold is used only for conclusions and recommendations in documents submitted for action.

IPBES practice:
- **Decisions.** The first word or words of each preambular and operative paragraph are in italics: "*Recalling* decision IPBES-11/1…", "*Welcomes*…", "*Requests* the Executive Secretary…", "*Also requests*…", "*Further invites*…". The Plenary text itself, "The Plenary,", is in roman type.
- **Report titles.** Titles of IPBES assessment reports are not italicized in Plenary reports; they are referred to descriptively in lower case. Italicize only when citing a published book or report as a publication, for example in a reference list.

### 4.5 Numbers, dates and time

UN rules:
- **Words or figures.**
  - Use words for one to nine.
  - Use figures for 10 and above, and always for percentages, measurements, ages, money, votes and ratios.
  - Never start a sentence with a figure; rewrite or use words.
  - Write simple fractions in words ("two thirds"; "two-thirds majority" as an adjective).
- **Series and adjacent numbers.**
  - In a series, the highest number decides the style: "8 of the 25 countries".
  - When two numbers are adjacent, put one in words.
- **Large numbers and decimals.**
  - In running text, use commas for thousands: "2,600", "$8,506,369".
  - In tables, use spaces: "8 506 369".
  - Use a decimal point, with a leading zero below 1: "0.5".
  - Write millions and billions as "1.5 million" and "$700 billion".
- **Percentages.** IPBES practice is "per cent" in running text (all reports; "%" does not appear in body text). Use "%" or "Percentage" in table headnotes only.
- **Currency.**
  - "$" means United States dollars. Write "$200,000", not "US$ 200,000" or "USD 200,000".
  - Other currencies: "€300,000", "£500,000".
  - Table headnote: "(United States dollars)".
- **Ranges.**
  - "from 3 to 8 February 2026"; "between 10 and 15"
  - "12–14 per cent" (en dash, no spaces)
  - "from 2024 to 2026", never "from 2024–2026"
  - the biennium "2025–2026"
  - Repeat the full figures: "from $2 million to $5 million".
- **Dates.**
  - Write day month year with no comma: "12 February 2026".
  - In running text: "from 3 to 8 February 2026".
  - In the corner notation: "3–8 February 2026" and "28 August–2 September 2023".
  - Do not write "this year" or "next year"; give the year.
  - "biannual" means twice a year and "biennial" means every two years; avoid both if possible.
- **Time.**
  - "10 a.m.", "9.55 a.m.", "12.50 p.m.", "noon", "midnight"
  - Not "10:00 a.m." or "10.00 a.m."
  - Ranges: "from 3 to 6 p.m.".
- **Ordinals.**
  - Use words up to ninety-ninth: "twelfth session", "fifteenth meeting of the Conference of the Parties".
  - Use figures for meetings of a session: "at its 2nd meeting".
  - Write centuries in words: "twenty-first century".
- **Roman numerals.** Use them for chapters, annexes and figures (up to 20): "annex II", "chapter III", "figure IV". Use Arabic numerals for tables: "table 1".
- **Session symbols.** Session references use Arabic numerals: IPBES/12/1, decision IPBES-12/1, IPBES-12. Ranges repeat the symbol: "decisions IPBES-12/1–IPBES-12/3". See `ipbes-terms.md` section 5.

### 4.6 Punctuation

UN rules:
- **Serial comma.** No serial comma: "FAO, UNDP, UNEP and UNESCO". Add one only where it is needed for clarity in long or complex lists.
- **Paired commas** must both be present: "In article 4, paragraph 6 (a), of the Convention…"; "At its 2nd meeting, on 4 February 2026, the Plenary…".
- **En dash (–).**
  - No spaces in ranges: "2024–2026", "pp. 17–19", "paras. 5–7".
  - Spaced, for an interruption: "– economic, social and environmental –".
  - Never use an em dash or a spaced hyphen ("-") as a dash.
- **Hyphen.** Use a hyphen for compounds as given in the spelling list: "decision-making", "capacity-building", "well-being", "Science-Policy", "non-governmental", "co-chair" (the noun is capitalized "Co-Chair").
- **Quotation marks and apostrophes** are curly (“ ” ‘ ’), never straight.
- **Apostrophes.** No apostrophe after acronyms, countries or bodies. Write "the IPBES secretariat", "the Government of Brazil", not "IPBES's", "Brazil's Government". Short forms may take one: "the Platform’s".
- **Colon.** A colon introduces a list. Lettered run-in lists use semicolons, with "and" or "or" before the last item and a full stop at the end.
- **Slash.** Avoid "and/or". Use "and" or "or". Slashes are fine in symbols (IPBES/12/1) and periods (2025/26).
- **Lists.**
  - Lettered subparagraphs end with ";", the second to last with "; and" or "; or", and the last with ".".
  - Bulleted short items take no punctuation; full sentences end with a full stop.
- **Spacing.**
  - One space after a full stop.
  - No space before ":" ";" "?" "!".
  - No double spaces.
- **Fixed spaces.** Use a non-breaking space between a figure and its unit ("9 km"), in "para. 7", "p. 12" and "10 a.m.". There is no space after "$", "€" or "£". Make sure symbols such as "IPBES/12/1" do not break across lines.

### 4.7 Quotations

UN rules:
- **Reproduce direct quotations exactly.** Permitted changes are limited to: the case of the initial letter; final punctuation; omitting footnotes; correcting obvious typos.
- **Sources.** Give the source of every quotation. For UN and IPBES documents, cite the paragraph, not the page.
- **Quotation marks.**
  - Use double marks “ ”, with single ‘ ’ for a quotation inside a quotation.
  - Use double marks for a specialized term at first introduction only.
- **Punctuation with quotations.**
  - A quoted fragment that is part of the sentence starts in lower case, with the final punctuation outside the marks.
  - A full-sentence quotation is introduced by a colon and starts with a capital, with the final full stop inside the marks.
- **Long quotations.** Quotations over five lines, or a full paragraph, are indented as a block, normally without quotation marks.
- **Omissions** are marked with an ellipsis (…), normally not at the start or end of a quotation.
- **Decisions and earlier reports.** Passages from decisions or earlier reports are normally paraphrased indirectly, without quotation marks.

### 4.8 Spelling

- **Authority.** British spelling with -ize per the Oxford Dictionary and the UN spelling list (`un-spelling-list.md`).
- **Correct forms:**
  - organization, programme (but computer "program"), centre, labour, behaviour, analyse, catalyse, synthesize, emphasize, recognize, prioritize, mobilize, judgement (general sense; "judgment" for court decisions), acknowledgement, ageing, towards, while (not "whilst"), among (not "amongst"), percentage, per cent.
- **Compounds found in IPBES reports and confirmed by the UN list:**
  - decision maker(s) (noun, open) and decision-making
  - policymaker(s) and policymaking
  - capacity-building (noun and adjective), but capacity development (noun)
  - well-being
  - intersessional
  - follow up (verb) and follow-up (noun and adjective)
  - land use (noun) and land-use (adjective)
  - freshwater (adjective)
  - groundwater
  - database and data set
  - multidisciplinary, multi-stakeholder, multilateral
  - non-governmental (non- compounds are hyphenated)
  - cooperation and coordination (no hyphen)
  - online, webinar, website, email
  - subregion and subregional
- **Not used:** "decision-makers", "policy makers", "wellbeing", "inter-sessional", "sub-regional", "co-operation", "e-mail".

## 5. Format

See `report-structure.md` for the masthead, Word styles, numbering and templates.

### 5.1 Headings and subheadings

- **Levels.** In the reports, CH1 is Roman numeral (14-point bold) and CH2 is a capital letter (12-point bold); both have a hanging indent with a tab before and after the number.
  - Levels below that: "1." (10-point bold), "(a)" (10-point bold), "(i)" (10-point italic).
  - Decision and annex titles are CH1 headings without a number.
- **Capitalization and punctuation.** Use sentence case: first word and proper nouns only. No full stop at the end. No abbreviations other than the document's own symbols.
- **Sequence and parallelism.**
  - Headings at the same level should be grammatically parallel.
  - Do not use a lone subheading: an "A" needs a "B", and a "1" needs a "2".
  - Check that numbering is sequential with no gaps: I, II, III… and A, B, C…
- **Subparagraphs** must not be interrupted by a heading.
- **Run-in headings** (bold, followed by a full stop) are allowed when each heading covers one paragraph.

### 5.2 Maps and figures, including photographs

- **Figures** (charts, graphs and photographs) are numbered in their own series with Roman numerals: "Figure I". Use Arabic numerals if there are more than 20. A single figure is not numbered.
- **Maps** are numbered in their own series with Arabic numerals. They need UN Geospatial Information Section clearance and the standard UN disclaimer: "The boundaries and names shown and the designations used on this map do not imply official endorsement or acceptance by the United Nations." Use the wording given in the cleared source.
- **Colour.** Masthead documents are printed in black and white, so figures must remain legible in greyscale.
- **Credits and permissions.** Photographs need a credit or source ("Source: …" or "Photo: …"). Do not use photographs of minors without permission.
- **Titles.** Figure titles are in sentence case.
- **Sources and notes.** Place these below the figure.

### 5.3 Mastheads and cover pages

See `report-structure.md` section 1. Check the masthead only when the document has one.

### 5.4 Paragraphs and subparagraphs

- **Paragraph numbering.** Number paragraphs consecutively with Arabic numerals (Word style Normal_number). Do not number:
  - summaries or front matter
  - the text of decisions in the report, which have their own numbered operative paragraphs
  - agenda lists, which use Normal_No_number with their own numbering
- **No empty paragraphs for spacing.** The IPBES styles set the space before and after each paragraph, heading and table title. Delete empty spacer paragraphs. Keep one only where two tables would otherwise merge.
- **Subparagraph sequence:** (a), (b), (c), then (i), (ii), (iii), then a., b., c., then i., ii., iii.
- **Lettered vs bulleted lists.** Prefer lettered subparagraphs to bullets, because they can be cross-referenced.
- **Chapeau and punctuation.** A chapeau ends with a colon. Each subparagraph ends with a semicolon, the second to last adds "and" or "or", and the last ends with a full stop.
- **Alternative and additional paragraphs** in negotiated texts use the bis, ter, quater sequence. In negotiated text, square brackets show unagreed text; flag them and do not remove them.

### 5.5 Tables

- **Number and title.**
  - Write "Table 1" (Arabic, flush left), followed by a bold title in sentence case on the next line.
  - The headnote goes in parentheses below the title: "(United States dollars)".
  - In annexes, numbering restarts at 1 for each annex, or use "Table A.1".
- **References in the text.** Every table must be cited in the text in lower case: "see table 1". If there is only one table, it has no number.
- **No abbreviations** in titles or headnotes. Write "Number" in full.
- **Rules and shading.** Use horizontal rules only: top, below the column heads, and bottom. No vertical rules, boxes or shading.
- **Headings.** Column heads are in italics and sentence case, and never vertical.
- **Alignment and figures.**
  - Align numbers on the decimal point or to the right. Text goes on the left.
  - Thousands take a space ("1 250 000").
  - Use the same number of decimal places down a column.
  - Negative amounts in financial tables go in parentheses.
- **Symbols:**
  - "–" means nil or negligible
  - ".." means not available
  - "(n/a)" means not applicable
  - no ditto marks
- **Totals.** Row totals are bold and indented. Column totals are in roman type.
- **Notes**, in this order:
  1. *Source:*
  2. *Note:*
  3. *Abbreviations:*
  4. footnotes marked with italic lower-case letters (a, b, c).

  Footnotes are phrases, not sentences.
- **Months** may be abbreviated in tables only: Jan., Feb., Mar., Apr., May, June, July, Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., Dec.
